FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about post-quantum cryptography and migration.

What is the quantum threat to modern cryptography?
↓
Quantum computers utilizing Shor’s algorithm can solve integer factorization and discrete logarithms in polynomial time, completely compromising public-key cryptography such as RSA, ECC, Diffie-Hellman, and DSA.
What is Harvest Now, Decrypt Later (HNDL)?
↓
Adversaries and state actors are actively recording encrypted network traffic today. Once sufficiently capable quantum computers emerge, they will decrypt this stored historical data. Any data with long-term confidentiality requirements is at risk right now.
Which algorithms are standardized by NIST?
↓
NIST finalized standards in 2024: ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key encapsulation, ML-DSA (FIPS 204) for digital signatures, and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) for stateless hash-based signatures. Qryptis recommends these standards.
How does Qryptis calculate the Cryptographic Agility Score?
↓
The score evaluates your code based on finding counts, prevalence of hardcoded deprecated ciphers, key size sufficiency, algorithm modularity, and readiness for drop-in PQC replacement libraries.
Can I export a Cryptography Bill of Materials (CBOM)?
↓
Yes. After running a code scan, you can download a standardized CycloneDX-compatible CBOM JSON file detailing all cryptographic assets, keys, and quantum vulnerability states.
